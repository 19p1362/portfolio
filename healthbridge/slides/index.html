The problem

Nothing is broken. Everything is disconnected.

  • The nurse writes a BP on a slip. The doctor asks for it again.
  • The prescription leaves as handwriting. The pharmacy guesses, then phones.
  • The follow-up date lives in a diary nobody outside that room can read.

Each handoff loses a fact. Multiply by 60 patients a day.

HealthBridge2 / 10
What it is

Three layers, one promise: the record follows the patient.

1 · Ingest

Photo or PDF of a prescription, lab report or discharge letter → structured fields.

OCR + extraction → FHIR resources

2 · Orchestrate

Ten care agents watch adherence, follow-ups, results and risk — and escalate to a human.

Agent loop + audit log

3 · Isolate

Every clinic is a tenant. One platform, no shared data, consent recorded per patient.

Tenant-scoped access · audit trail

The differentiator is not the model. It is that step 3 keeps the patient's data with the patient's clinic.

UK adoption reality

What selling into the NHS actually requires.

Gate 1 — organisational

  • DTAC (Digital Technology Assessment Criteria) evidence pack
  • DSPT toolkit submission, annually renewed
  • UK GDPR + Data Protection Impact Assessment
  • Cyber Essentials / ISO 27001 expectation

Gate 2 — clinical safety

  • DCB0129 clinical risk management (manufacturer)
  • Hazard log, clinical safety case report, named Clinical Safety Officer
  • Human-in-the-loop before anything enters the record

not claimed yet  We have none of these badges. We have the architecture that makes them answerable — tenant isolation, audit trail, no ambient data access.

HealthBridge · UK framing7 / 10
Boundaries

What HealthBridge does not do.

Not an EPR

It does not replace EMIS Web or SystmOne. It reads, proposes, and exports back.

Not a diagnosis

No autonomous clinical decision. A clinician confirms every code and every escalation.

Not a data lake

Nothing leaves a tenant. Each clinic's patients stay inside that clinic's boundary.

Stating limits early is what makes the rest believable — and it is the first thing a UK buyer tests.
